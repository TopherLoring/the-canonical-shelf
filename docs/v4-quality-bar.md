# v4 quality bar

The redesign should not merely be “more polished than v3.” It should feel like a coherent learning product built around the material.

## Copy

- No generic filler such as “use this title as a retrieval anchor.”
- No learner-facing references to “legacy steps,” “old tracks,” migration, or implementation architecture.
- Every explanation should flow naturally from the previous idea and make sense to a beginner without assuming church vocabulary.
- Define a term when it first becomes necessary, not in a detached glossary dump.
- Plain-English summaries should clarify, not merely shorten.
- Disagreement should be represented in terms adherents would recognize.
- When evidence is uncertain, the wording must remain uncertain.

## Visuals

- A visual earns its place by carrying relationships, sequence, scale, comparison, or structure that prose handles less efficiently.
- Avoid decorative illustration as a substitute for teaching.
- Every visual gets a meaningful accessible label/caption.
- Timelines distinguish approximate from anchored dates.
- Maps are explicitly schematic unless geographic precision is sourced.
- Comparison visuals must not imply two positions are equally common, equally evidenced, or morally equivalent merely because they occupy symmetrical columns.

## Games and checks

- Game mechanics must correspond to the skill being tested.
- Reordering tests sequence; matching tests conceptual association; evidence boards test calibration; argument maps test reasoning; scenarios test transfer; verse rebuild tests recall/context.
- Wrong-answer feedback should explain the distinction the learner missed.
- Hints should progressively reduce difficulty rather than instantly reveal the answer.
- Native `<select>` controls are not the primary interaction surface for course games.
- Keyboard interaction is a first-class path, not a fallback.
- Motion is restrained and disabled under reduced-motion preference.
- Completion should feel satisfying without fake currency or manipulative streak pressure.

## Overall information architecture

- Learn = sequenced course.
- Practice = targeted retrieval/review and book/canon drills.
- Explore = Bible/library browsing.
- Verses = curated passage library and context.
- Topics = expert-style plain-English reference desk.

The same information may be linked across surfaces, but each surface has a distinct job.
